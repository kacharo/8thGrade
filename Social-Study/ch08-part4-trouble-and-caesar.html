💡 Big Idea: Rich landowners, poor farmers, and powerful armies caused trouble. Julius Caesar took power and made changes.

1📖 The Story in Small BitesTap 🔊 to listen

🌾

Farmers in Trouble

Rich Romans bought land to make latifundia — huge farms worked by enslaved people. Small farmers could not compete. Many lost their farms and moved to crowded cities with no jobs.

🎭

Bread and Circuses

Leaders gave poor people free food and fun shows like chariot races to keep them happy — and to get their votes.

🤝

The Gracchus Brothers

Tiberius and Gaius Gracchus tried to give land to poor Romans. Many senators hated their ideas. Both brothers were killed.

💵

Armies Change

A general named Marius began paying poor men to be soldiers. Soon soldiers were loyal to their general, not to Rome. Generals like Sulla used their armies to grab power.

3️⃣

The First Triumvirate

Julius Caesar, Pompey, and Crassus formed a triumvirate — three men sharing power. Caesar became a hero by conquering Gaul (France).

🌉

Crossing the Rubicon

In 49 B.C., the Senate told Caesar to give up his army. Instead, he crossed the Rubicon River into Italy and started a civil war. Today 'crossing the Rubicon' means making a choice you can't take back.

🗡️

Caesar's Changes and Death

Caesar made himself dictator for life. He gave land to the poor and made the 365-day calendar with a leap year. Some senators feared he wanted to be king. On March 15, 44 B.C. — the Ides of March — they killed him.
