The Byzantine Church & the Great Split

📚 Textbook pages 358–361

💡 Big Idea: In the Byzantine Empire, church and government worked together. Arguments with Rome led to a split in 1054.

1📖 The Story in Small BitesTap 🔊 to listen

🏛️

Two Halves

The Western Roman Empire fell, but the East kept going as the Byzantine Empire. Its Greek-based church was the Eastern Orthodox Church. In the West, it was the Roman Catholic Church.

👑

Emperor in Charge

Byzantines believed their emperor represented Jesus on Earth. The emperor chose the patriarch of Constantinople, so he controlled both church and government.

🖼️

The Icon Fight

Icons are pictures of Jesus, Mary, and the saints. Many people honored them. In A.D. 726, Emperor Leo III ordered icons removed. The officials who did it were called iconoclasts — image breakers. Later, icons were allowed again.

😠

Who Is the Boss?

The pope said he was head of ALL churches. The Byzantines said the patriarch was equal to the pope. In A.D. 800, the pope crowned the Frankish king Charlemagne emperor. The Byzantines were angry.

✂️

The Split of 1054

In A.D. 1054, the pope and the patriarch excommunicated each other. This caused a schism, or split. The Roman Catholic and Eastern Orthodox Churches are still separate today.
