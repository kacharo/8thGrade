💡 Big Idea: Some emperors were cruel, some were good. Roads, aqueducts, ports, and one shared currency made the empire rich.

1📖 The Story in Small BitesTap 🔊 to listen

😈

Bad Emperors

After Augustus, some emperors were cruel. Caligula was so strange that, the story goes, he planned to make his horse a consul! Nero killed many people, including his own mother.

😇

The Good Emperors

Starting in A.D. 96, five 'Good Emperors' ruled for nearly 100 years: Nerva, Trajan, Hadrian, Antoninus Pius, and Marcus Aurelius. Under Trajan, the empire was biggest. Hadrian built walls to protect it and made laws easier to understand.

🗺️

A Giant Empire

The empire stretched from Britain to Egypt and had about 3.5 million square miles. Rivers like the Rhine and Danube formed its borders in Europe.

🛣️

Roads and Aqueducts

Rome built about 50,000 miles of stone roads. Aqueducts carried fresh water from the hills into cities.

🪙

Trade and Money

Ports like Ostia and Puteoli were busy with ships. Everyone used the same currency (money), which made trade easy. Silk came from China and spices from India.

🧑‍⚖️

Citizens and Laws

Over time, most people in the empire became citizens. They followed the same Roman laws, which helped unite the empire.
