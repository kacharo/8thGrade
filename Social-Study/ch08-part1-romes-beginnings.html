💡 Big Idea: Geography helped Rome grow. The Romans set up a republic and slowly took over all of Italy.

1📖 The Story in Small BitesTap 🔊 to listen

🥾

A Boot-Shaped Land

Italy is a peninsula shaped like a tall boot. It sticks out into the Mediterranean Sea. The island of Sicily sits by the toe of the boot.

⛰️

Easy Mountains

The Apennine Mountains run down the middle of Italy. They are not as steep as the mountains in Greece, so people could farm and travel more easily.

🌊

A Smart Spot for a City

Rome was built on seven hills along the Tiber River, about 15 miles from the sea. The hills were easy to defend. The river gave water and a way to trade. Being away from the coast kept Rome safer from pirates.

🐺

Two Legends

One legend says twin brothers Romulus and Remus were saved by a wolf and built Rome in 753 B.C. Another legend says Aeneas, a hero from Troy, came to Italy and his people became the Romans. Historians think the Latins lived near Rome as early as 1000 B.C.

🏺

The Etruscans

The Etruscans came from Etruria, north of Rome. They ruled Rome around 650 B.C. They turned Rome from a village of straw huts into a city of wood and brick buildings. Romans also copied their clothing, the toga.

🗳️

Rome Becomes a Republic

In 509 B.C., the Romans threw out Tarquin, the last Etruscan king. They made a republic, where citizens choose their leaders.

🛡️

Conquering Italy

Rome's army was organized into legions of about 6,000 soldiers. By 267 B.C. Rome controlled most of Italy. Rome treated conquered people fairly. Some got full citizenship, and others became allies. This kept them loyal.
