💡 Big Idea: Rome's republic was a struggle between rich landowners and regular citizens. Over time, more people got the right to vote.

1📖 The Story in Small BitesTap 🔊 to listen

💰

Two Groups of Citizens

Patricians were rich landowners and the ruling class. Plebeians were regular people like farmers, shopkeepers, and workers. Both could vote, pay taxes, and serve in the army.

👥

Two Consuls

The top leaders were two consuls, chosen every year. Each consul could veto (reject) the other's decision. The word veto is Latin for 'I forbid.'

⚖️

Judges and the Senate

Praetors were judges. The Senate was a group of 300 patrician men who advised the leaders and made laws.

✊

Plebeians Fight Back

Plebeians were unhappy that patricians had more power. They went on strike and refused to fight. Finally they got their own Council of the Plebs and leaders called tribunes.

⏳

A Dictator for Emergencies

In a crisis, Rome could choose a dictator with total power — but only for six months. Cincinnatus was a farmer who became dictator, beat Rome's enemy, and went right back to his farm after just 16 days.

📜

The Twelve Tables

In 451 B.C., Rome wrote its first laws on twelve bronze tablets: the Twelve Tables. Everyone could see the laws, and the laws applied to everyone. This is called the rule of law.
